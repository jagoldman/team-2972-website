About This Handbook

This handbook describes the enduring identity, expectations, organization, and operating principles of FIRST Robotics Competition Team 2972 at Alexander Dawson School. It is intended to remain useful across seasons. Season-specific details belong in a separate annual team guide.

This handbook defines the program: its mission, values, organization, and expectations. It names the systems the team uses and the expectations for using them; step-by-step instructions belong in onboarding and app help documentation.

Students lead the work and remain accountable for their commitments. Mentors teach, facilitate, supervise, and provide continuity. Safety and demonstrated competency govern shop access.

CriticalSchool policy comes first

This handbook supplements, and does not supersede, Alexander Dawson School policies and applicable FIRST requirements.

Program principles

  • Mission: We build people through robotics.
  • Team model: Students learn by doing meaningful work on a real engineering and competition program.
  • Culture: Team First, Shared Knowledge, Ownership, Continuous Improvement, Gracious Professionalism, and Psychological Safety and Respect.
  • Leadership: Leadership is a coordinating responsibility across the program, not simply another technical specialty.
  • Participation: Students are encouraged to contribute consistently, communicate constraints early, and follow through on commitments. This handbook does not set a universal attendance quota unless the school and team formally adopt one.
  • Accountability: Problems are addressed early and proportionately. Serious safety, harassment, or conduct concerns may require immediate intervention under school policy.
  • Continuity: Decisions, designs, processes, and lessons are documented so that knowledge belongs to the team rather than a single student or mentor.
01

Welcome to Team 2972

Team 2972, the Gears & Buccaneers, is Alexander Dawson School's FIRST Robotics Competition team. We design, build, program, test, operate, and compete with a robot while developing the skills and judgment needed to work as a team. Students also run the program behind the robot: sponsorship and fundraising, budgeting, outreach, media, and FIRST awards submissions.

Robotics is the setting; student development is the purpose. Members learn technical skills, communication, project management, problem-solving, leadership, and responsibility by contributing to a complex shared effort.

The team participates in the FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC). FIRST provides the framework for the competition, while the team is responsible for building a sustainable program that supports students across seasons.

1.1Mission

We build people through robotics.

We aim to create a challenging, safe, and supportive environment in which students can develop confidence, competence, curiosity, and the ability to work with others. We want students to leave the program better prepared to contribute to their communities and to pursue future opportunities in STEM and beyond.

1.2What success means

Competitive results matter, but they are not the only measure of success. A strong season also means:

  • Students gain skills and become more independent.
  • Students can explain the decisions behind their work.
  • Knowledge is shared and documented.
  • The robot and team systems improve through testing and reflection.
  • Members contribute to a respectful, inclusive team culture.
  • The program becomes more capable and sustainable for the students who follow.
02

Team Culture and Values

The team is student-centered and learning-focused. Students are expected to take meaningful ownership, while mentors provide instruction, support, and appropriate supervision.

2.1Team First

The team's goals take priority over individual recognition. Members should share information, help teammates succeed, and make decisions that support the broader program.

2.2Shared Knowledge

Knowledge belongs to the team. Students and mentors should teach others, document important processes, and avoid creating unnecessary dependence on a single person.

A member who learns a skill should look for opportunities to help another member learn it. Documentation, demonstrations, and repeatable processes make the team stronger.

2.3Ownership

Students should take responsibility for their work from understanding the problem through design, execution, testing, and documentation. Ownership includes communicating progress, asking for help, identifying risks, and following through.

Ownership does not mean working alone. Asking for help is a sign of sound judgment.

2.4Continuous Improvement

The team should test assumptions, learn from failures, and make changes based on evidence. Mistakes are part of learning; hiding mistakes or failing to communicate them can create safety and project risks.

After important work or events, the team should reflect on what worked, what did not, and what should change.

2.5Gracious Professionalism

Team 2972 follows the FIRST ethos of Gracious Professionalism: competing hard and doing high-quality work while treating others with respect and kindness. Members should treat teammates, mentors, school staff, volunteers, event personnel, and other teams with respect. Gracious Professionalism includes being prepared, communicating clearly, caring for shared equipment, and representing the school and team responsibly.

2.6Psychological Safety and Respect

Members should be able to ask questions, admit uncertainty, propose ideas, and report problems without fear of humiliation. Criticism should focus on the work, decision, or behavior, not on a person's worth or identity.

CriticalHarassment and bullying

Harassment, bullying, discrimination, intimidation, and retaliation are not acceptable. School policies and reporting procedures apply.

03

Organization and Leadership

Team 2972 uses divisions to represent the major areas of work required to operate the program. Divisions provide coordination and visibility; they are not rigid silos. Students may contribute across divisions and projects as their interests and skills develop.

3.1Team Divisions

Engineering

Responsible for designing, building, wiring, programming, integrating, and testing the robot and related technical systems.

  • Mechanical design and fabrication
  • CAD and engineering drawings
  • Electrical design, wiring, and controls
  • Robot software and autonomous routines
  • Sensors, pneumatics, and mechanisms
  • System integration, testing, and reliability

Competition

Responsible for preparing the team and robot to compete effectively and safely.

  • Game analysis and strategy
  • Match scouting and data analysis
  • Drive team preparation and practice
  • Robot operation and maintenance
  • Pit operations and match readiness
  • Event planning and competition logistics

Outreach & Fundraising

Responsible for connecting the team with the broader community and securing support for the program.

  • Community demonstrations and outreach
  • Sponsor relationships and fundraising
  • Marketing, media, and team communications
  • Awards preparation and storytelling
  • Community partnerships and recruitment

Team Operations

Responsible for the systems and resources that allow the team to work effectively.

  • Shop organization and cleanliness
  • Inventory, purchasing, and parts requests
  • Budget tracking and resource planning
  • Documentation and records
  • Event logistics and equipment preparation
  • Digital infrastructure and administrative processes

Leadership

Responsible for coordination across the team and helping the program operate as a coherent whole.

  • Team-wide planning and communication
  • Coordinating divisions and projects
  • Helping establish priorities and resolve conflicts
  • Supporting accountability and follow-through
  • Developing team culture and future leaders
  • Maintaining continuity across seasons

Leadership is not a separate claim to authority over every task. It is a responsibility to help the team coordinate, communicate, and succeed.

3.2Projects and Working Groups

The team organizes much of its work around projects. A project is a defined effort with an objective, an owner or lead, a working group, and a set of tasks and milestones.

A project may contain working groups or subteams such as CAD, mechanical, electrical, programming, fabrication, or scouting. These groups are formed around the work needed; they do not have to be permanent divisions.

Students may join multiple projects and contribute in more than one area. Assignments should reflect student interest, learning goals, availability, and the needs of the team.

3.3Student Leadership

Student leaders help the team plan and coordinate work. Their responsibilities may include:

  • Communicating priorities and expectations
  • Helping divide projects into achievable tasks
  • Checking progress and identifying blockers
  • Encouraging participation and shared learning
  • Coordinating with other divisions
  • Bringing unresolved issues to mentors

Leadership roles and assignments may change from season to season. The annual team guide should identify current student leaders and their responsibilities.

3.4Coach and Mentors

The Head Coach is responsible for the overall operation of the team, student supervision, coordination with the school, season planning, and major program decisions.

Mentors contribute technical knowledge, instruction, project guidance, student development, and program continuity. Mentors help students understand problems and make sound decisions rather than simply completing work for them.

Key pointAdults stay responsible for safety

The Coach and mentors retain responsibility for appropriate adult supervision and safety decisions. Student leadership does not replace adult responsibility.

04

How We Work

4.1Start with the Objective

Before beginning work, the team should understand:

  • What problem are we trying to solve?
  • What does a successful result look like?
  • What constraints apply?
  • Who needs to be involved?
  • How will we test or verify the result?

A clear objective helps prevent wasted effort and makes it easier for students to make decisions independently.

4.2Plan Work and Make Commitments

Projects should be broken into tasks that have clear outcomes and, where useful, an owner and due date. Larger efforts should have milestones so the team can see whether the project is on track.

Key pointWhat a commitment means

Members should:

  • Understand what they have agreed to do.
  • Communicate when a task is unclear or blocked.
  • Raise schedule or resource conflicts early.
  • Update the team when work is complete.
  • Avoid silently abandoning assignments.

Task assignments are tools for coordination and learning, not a measure of a student's worth.

4.3Ask for Help Early

Students should try to understand a problem and make a reasonable attempt, but should not remain stuck indefinitely. Ask a teammate or mentor when requirements are unclear, a decision has significant consequences, or progress is blocked.

CriticalStop unsafe work

Safety concerns must be raised immediately. Do not continue unsafe work while waiting for help.

4.4Design, Build, Test, Learn

The team should use an iterative engineering process:

  1. Understand the problem and constraints.
  2. Generate and compare possible solutions.
  3. Design and document the selected approach.
  4. Build or implement the solution.
  5. Test against the intended requirements.
  6. Record results and make improvements.

A completed task is not necessarily a successful solution. Testing and verification are part of the work.

4.5Documentation and Handoffs

Important work should be documented well enough that another team member can understand, maintain, or continue it. This includes CAD, drawings, code, wiring information, test results, operating procedures, and project decisions.

When handing work to another person, explain the current state, relevant decisions, known problems, and next steps.

4.6Digital Infrastructure and Team Applications

Team 2972 uses a set of digital tools to coordinate work, communicate, design, document, and compete. The custom 2972 Team Portal is the central home and access point for team applications.

The portal and its applications support team operations. They do not replace direct communication, adult supervision, or good judgment.

The 2972 Team Portal

The portal is the team's access point for its applications, and you can download every application through it. It is expected to grow as the team's needs change.

Tasks

The custom Tasks app is the primary source of tasks for the team. It organizes projects, working groups, and milestones. Students should use it to understand current priorities, take responsibility for assigned work, communicate blockers, and keep project information current.

The Tasks app supports coordination; it is not intended to turn every learning activity into a rigid checklist. Mentors and student leaders should use it to make work visible and manageable while leaving room for exploration and student initiative.

Design, Software, and Shared Files

  • Onshape is used for robot CAD and collaborative mechanical design.
  • GitHub is used for robot code and software collaboration.
  • Team Drive is used for shared team files and documents.

Members should use the team's established shared spaces rather than keeping essential project information only in personal accounts or devices.

Training and Skills

FIRST training resources are available through the FIRST Dashboard. The team also uses its internal Skills system to track training progress and mentor sign-offs for team-specific skills and equipment qualifications.

Training records help mentors and students understand readiness. They do not override equipment restrictions, supervision requirements, or school policy.

Other Team Applications

The portal may also provide access to other applications that support team operations, such as attendance, inventory, scheduling, and scouting.

The applications and their features may evolve. The annual team guide and onboarding materials should explain current procedures. This handbook defines the team's expectations, not the detailed operation of each application.

Digital Responsibilities

Members are expected to:

  • Use team systems for their intended purposes.
  • Keep project information accurate and useful.
  • Communicate blockers and important changes.
  • Protect account credentials and respect access permissions.
  • Keep shared files organized.
  • Use professional language and judgment in digital spaces.
  • Avoid sharing private student, family, or school information without authorization.
05

Student Development and Technical Training

The team is committed to helping students develop both breadth and depth. Every student should have opportunities to learn the fundamentals, explore areas of interest, and develop meaningful expertise.

5.1A Common Foundation

Students should develop a baseline understanding of:

  • Shop safety and personal protective equipment
  • Basic hand tools and common shop equipment
  • Measurement, layout, and basic fabrication
  • Fasteners and mechanical assembly
  • Basic electrical concepts and safe wiring practices
  • Basic programming and software collaboration
  • Team organization, documentation, and communication

The precise training path will depend on student experience, project needs, and available instruction.

5.2Learning by Doing

Training should connect to real team work. Students learn through instruction, demonstrations, guided practice, independent practice where authorized, and reflection.

A student should be able to explain not only how to perform a task, but why the method is appropriate and what risks or limitations apply.

5.3Skills and Sign-Off

The team's Skills system supports a progression from introductory learning to demonstrated competency. Safety training is foundational. Additional skills may have prerequisites and may require a written check, practical demonstration, design review, or a combination.

Students may request a mentor sign-off when they believe they are ready. A mentor may approve the skill or identify what additional practice is needed.

CriticalSign-offs come from mentors

Students may not sign off their own skills. A recorded qualification is not permission to ignore posted restrictions, equipment instructions, or mentor direction.

5.4Equipment Authorization

CriticalNo sign-off, no independent machine use

Some equipment requires documented training and mentor sign-off before a student may operate it independently. The team maintains an equipment authorization record for applicable tools.

The current equipment authorization board and mentor direction determine whether a student may operate a particular machine and under what conditions. When in doubt, stop and ask.

Authorization is specific to the equipment and conditions described by the team. It does not automatically transfer to another machine or process.

5.5Teaching Others

Students who become proficient should help teach newer members. Teaching reinforces understanding and makes the program less dependent on a small number of experienced people.

Students should not train others to operate equipment beyond their own authorization. Mentors remain responsible for ensuring that instruction and supervision are appropriate.

06

Shop Safety and Equipment

Critical section. Read every part.

CriticalSafety is a condition of participation

Safety is a condition of participation, not a trade-off against speed or productivity. Every member is responsible for protecting themselves and others.

This section establishes team expectations. It does not replace equipment manuals, posted procedures, school safety requirements, or direct mentor instruction. When rules conflict, follow the more restrictive applicable requirement and ask the responsible adult.

6.1Personal Protective Equipment and Conduct

Unless a mentor or posted procedure specifies additional protection:

  • Wear safety glasses in shop and fabrication areas.
  • Wear closed-toe shoes.
  • Secure long hair, loose clothing, jewelry, and anything that could become caught.
  • Do not wear gloves near rotating machinery.
  • Use hearing, respiratory, or other protection when required.
  • Keep walkways and exits clear.

Members must know the locations of emergency stops, eyewash, first-aid supplies, and other relevant safety equipment.

6.2General Machine Rules

  • Operate equipment only after receiving the required training and permission.
  • Use guards, clamps, and workholding as instructed.
  • Never bypass safety devices.
  • Keep hands clear of moving and cutting areas.
  • Stop the machine before making adjustments or clearing material.
  • Do not leave equipment running unless the specific process and team rules permit it.
  • Do not distract someone operating machinery.
  • Report damaged equipment, unsafe conditions, and near misses.
  • Return tools and clean the work area after use.

CriticalWhen uncertain, stop

A student must stop work and ask for help whenever they are uncertain about a machine, setup, material, or procedure.

6.3Equipment-Specific Expectations

The team maintains equipment-specific training and authorization for tools such as the compound miter saw, drill press, bandsaw, lathe, CNC router, and laser.

Examples of critical precautions include:

  • Miter saw: Use the guard and appropriate clamping; keep hands outside the marked danger area.
  • Drill press: Secure the workpiece, use appropriate speed and setup, and keep hands and loose items away from rotating parts.
  • Lathe: Remove the chuck key immediately; never leave it in the chuck. Secure work correctly and follow the required setup procedure.
  • Laser: Keep the lid closed, follow material restrictions, remain present as required, and stop the job if flame or smoke appears. Never process prohibited materials.
  • CNC router: Use approved setups, tooling, workholding, and files. Confirm the work area is clear before starting.

Key pointThis handbook is not a permission slip

The equipment authorization board and current training materials contain the applicable operating permissions and restrictions. This handbook does not grant permission to operate any machine.

6.4Fire, Smoke, and Emergencies

CriticalIn an emergency

If a machine or process produces unexpected flame, smoke, or another emergency:

  1. Stop the process if it is safe to do so.
  2. Notify a mentor or responsible adult immediately.
  3. Follow the adult's instructions and evacuate if the situation is spreading or cannot be controlled safely.
  4. Do not attempt to fight a battery fire or handle a hazardous situation beyond your training.

Report every injury, even if it appears minor. Follow school emergency procedures and adult direction.

6.5Batteries and Electrical Safety

  • Inspect batteries for damage, swelling, cracks, loose terminals, or other abnormalities.
  • Carry batteries by their cases and use the designated storage and charging areas.
  • Do not use a damaged battery; set it aside only if safe and notify a mentor.
  • Do not leave batteries charging overnight.
  • Keep battery terminals protected from accidental shorts.
  • Do not perform exposed high-current electrical repairs unless specifically authorized and supervised.
  • Before working on the robot, disable it, turn off the main breaker, and disconnect the battery as appropriate.
  • Before enabling the robot, clear the area, announce the action, and ensure someone is ready to disable it.

6.6Robot Movement and Operation

Moving the robot requires an appropriate cart and the team's established method. Use two people when required by the team procedure. Keep the path clear and communicate before moving.

CriticalBefore enabling the robot

  • Confirm that people are clear of mechanisms and moving parts.
  • Announce that the robot is about to be enabled.
  • Ensure a responsible person can disable it promptly.
  • Stop immediately if someone enters the work area or calls for a stop.

6.7Housekeeping

At the end of work:

  • Turn off equipment as required.
  • Return tools and materials to their designated locations.
  • Dispose of scrap and waste appropriately.
  • Sweep and clear work surfaces.
  • Leave the shop ready for the next group.

A clean and organized shop is a safety requirement and a shared responsibility.

07

Participation and Attendance

The team depends on members communicating and following through. Participation is not measured only by hours spent in the shop; the quality, reliability, and learning value of a member's contribution also matter.

7.1Participation Expectations

Members should:

  • Attend activities they have committed to whenever possible.
  • Arrive prepared to participate.
  • Communicate absences, late arrivals, and schedule conflicts as early as practical.
  • Keep project teammates informed when availability changes.
  • Complete agreed work or communicate when it cannot be completed.
  • Respect the time and effort of teammates and mentors.

The annual team guide should provide the current practice schedule and season-specific expectations.

7.2Attendance Records

The team may use its attendance application to record participation and help with planning. Members should follow the current sign-in and sign-out procedure.

If a record is missing or incorrect, tell a mentor so it can be reviewed. Attendance records are intended to support accurate coordination and reporting.

7.3No Substitute for Communication

Key pointTell a person, not just the app

A digital attendance record does not replace telling the responsible person about an absence, schedule change, or safety concern. Members should communicate directly when a commitment or responsibility is affected.

7.4School Responsibilities

Key pointSchool responsibilities come first

Students are expected to maintain their academic and school responsibilities. Team participation should not come at the expense of meeting school expectations. Students who are struggling to balance commitments should speak with a mentor or the Coach.

08

Competition Operations

Competition is a major part of FRC, but successful events depend on preparation, teamwork, and disciplined execution, not only on the robot.

The annual team guide should contain the current event calendar, travel arrangements, roster, event assignments, and season-specific procedures.

8.1Preparation

Before an event, the team should:

  • Confirm robot readiness and inspection requirements.
  • Prepare tools, spare parts, batteries, chargers, and pit equipment.
  • Review the game, strategy, and event schedule.
  • Confirm student and mentor roles.
  • Prepare scouting and communication plans.
  • Ensure students understand event expectations and safety procedures.

Competition responsibilities should be practiced before the event rather than introduced for the first time at the venue.

8.2Event Roles

Depending on the event and team needs, students may serve in roles such as:

  • Drive team
  • Pit crew
  • Scouting
  • Strategy support
  • Media and outreach
  • Team leadership and coordination

Roles should be communicated clearly. Students should understand where they need to be, when they need to be there, and whom to contact if a problem arises.

8.3Pit Operations

The pit is a shared technical workspace and public representation of the team. Members should keep it organized, maintain safe working conditions, and communicate clearly.

Before a match, the team should use its current pit and robot-readiness checklist. Checks may include:

  • Battery installation and secure mounting
  • Bumpers and team numbers
  • Radio and communications status
  • Mechanical fasteners and moving mechanisms
  • Electrical connections and battery condition
  • Software configuration and autonomous selection
  • Known issues from the previous match

Key pointNever hide a problem

Any identified problem should be communicated and tracked until resolved. Do not conceal a problem to avoid delaying a match.

8.4Scouting and Strategy

Scouting provides information that supports match strategy and alliance selection. Students should collect observations accurately, submit reports promptly, and distinguish observed facts from assumptions.

Strategy decisions should use available evidence while recognizing uncertainty. Students should treat other teams respectfully and avoid misrepresenting their capabilities.

8.5Drive Team and Robot Operation

Drive team members must be trained and selected through the team's current process. They are responsible for operating the robot safely, following field and event staff instructions, and communicating with the pit crew and mentors.

Before a robot is enabled or moved, the area must be clear and the team must follow its established safety procedure. Any member may call attention to an unsafe condition.

8.6Match Debriefs and Improvement

After matches, the team should review performance using observations, video, logs, and relevant data. Debriefs should identify a small number of actionable improvements.

The goal is to learn and improve, not to assign blame. The team should record important findings so that the same problems are not repeatedly rediscovered.

8.7Event Conduct

CriticalConduct at events and while traveling

Members represent Alexander Dawson School and Team 2972 throughout an event, including travel, hotels, venues, and interactions with other teams. Follow school and FIRST rules, respect event staff, and maintain appropriate conduct.

09

Communication and Digital Professionalism

Clear communication is essential to a student-led team. Members should use the appropriate channel, provide enough context for others to act, and communicate changes before they create problems.

9.1Team Channels

Team 2972 uses several communication channels for different purposes:

  • Discord: High-frequency, day-to-day team communication and coordination.
  • Email: General announcements, newsletter updates, and broader communications with students and families.
  • 2972 Team Portal: Access to team applications and operational information.
  • Tasks app: Project and task status, ownership, milestones, and blockers.
  • Onshape, GitHub, and Team Drive: Technical collaboration, version history, and shared project files.

The annual team guide and onboarding materials should identify current channel conventions and access procedures.

9.2Communication Expectations

Members should:

  • Read and respond to messages relevant to their responsibilities.
  • Use clear, respectful language.
  • Include enough context for others to understand the request.
  • Communicate blockers and schedule conflicts promptly.
  • Avoid relying on a private message when the information affects the whole project.
  • Keep technical decisions and important project information in shared, accessible locations.

Key pointUrgent issues go to an adult directly

A message being sent does not guarantee that it has been seen. For urgent safety or event issues, communicate directly with the responsible mentor or adult.

9.3Digital Conduct

CriticalOnline conduct

The same standards of respect and professionalism apply online as in person. Do not use team channels to harass, threaten, demean, or exclude others. Do not share private information, images, or school-related information without appropriate permission.

Members should protect their accounts and use team systems responsibly. Report suspicious access, inappropriate content, or misuse to a mentor or the Coach.

10

Conduct, Accountability, and Conflict Resolution

Critical section. Read every part.

A productive team requires trust, respect, and accountability. Members are expected to contribute to an environment where people can learn and work safely.

10.1Expected Conduct

Members should:

  • Treat others with respect.
  • Follow mentor and school staff directions, especially regarding safety.
  • Use tools, materials, and facilities responsibly.
  • Be honest about mistakes and project status.
  • Respect shared property and other people's work.
  • Avoid behavior that interferes with another person's ability to participate.
  • Represent the team and school appropriately.

10.2Addressing Problems

When a problem arises, members should address it early and constructively when safe and appropriate. A student may speak with a teammate, student leader, mentor, or the Coach.

CriticalSerious concerns go to an adult

Concerns involving safety, harassment, bullying, discrimination, retaliation, or adult misconduct should be brought to a responsible adult or reported through school procedures. Students do not need to resolve serious concerns directly with the person involved.

10.3Accountability and Response

The team should respond to problems in a way that is fair, proportionate, and focused on safety, learning, and restoring trust where possible.

Responses may include:

  • Clarifying expectations or providing additional instruction
  • A conversation with a mentor or the Coach
  • A plan for improvement or repair
  • Temporary limits on a task, tool, or activity
  • Involving school administration or following school disciplinary procedures

CriticalSchool procedures govern

Serious safety or conduct concerns may require immediate intervention. The school's policies and procedures govern where applicable. This handbook does not promise a particular sequence of disciplinary steps or limit the school's authority.

10.4Conflict Resolution

When disagreements occur:

  1. Focus on the issue and the evidence, not personal attacks.
  2. Give each person an opportunity to explain their perspective.
  3. Identify the shared objective or constraint.
  4. Agree on a decision, next step, or person responsible for resolving the issue.
  5. Ask a mentor or the Coach to facilitate when the disagreement cannot be resolved constructively.

Disagreement about technical choices is normal and can improve the robot. Disrespectful behavior is not an acceptable part of that process.

11

Mentors, Volunteers, and Families

The team is supported by students, school staff, mentors, volunteers, and families. Each group contributes to a safe and sustainable program.

11.1Mentors and Volunteers

Mentors provide instruction, technical guidance, project support, and encouragement. They help students develop the ability to solve problems and take responsibility.

Mentors are expected to:

  • Model respectful and professional conduct.
  • Support the team's student-centered philosophy.
  • Teach rather than unnecessarily take over.
  • Maintain appropriate boundaries.
  • Follow school and FIRST requirements.
  • Enforce safety expectations consistently.
  • Communicate concerns to the Coach.
  • Help preserve knowledge and continuity.

Mentors must follow all applicable school requirements for volunteer screening, training, supervision, and student interaction. Current requirements should be confirmed with the school and documented in annual onboarding materials.

11.2Families

Families support students by helping them manage commitments, communicating relevant schedule constraints, and reinforcing the values of learning, responsibility, and respect.

Families may contribute through volunteering, event support, outreach, fundraising, transportation where authorized, and other program needs. Specific opportunities and procedures should be communicated in the annual team guide.

Key pointFor parents and guardians

Parents and guardians should direct program questions or concerns to the Coach or designated school contact rather than relying on students to resolve adult-level issues.

11.3School Partnership

CriticalSchool policies take precedence

Team 2972 operates within Alexander Dawson School. School policies, supervision requirements, facility rules, and administrative decisions take precedence over this handbook.

The Coach coordinates with the school regarding program operations, student safety, facilities, volunteers, travel, and other institutional requirements.

A Commitment to the Team

Being part of Team 2972 means contributing to something larger than an individual project or competition result. Each member helps create the environment in which others learn, build, and succeed.

We ask every student to

  • Learn with curiosity.
  • Work safely.
  • Take ownership.
  • Share knowledge.
  • Communicate honestly.
  • Respect others.
  • Follow through on commitments.
  • Help make the team better for the next person.

We ask every mentor and volunteer to

  • Put student development first.
  • Teach and guide with patience and consistency.
  • Maintain safe and respectful working conditions.
  • Support student ownership.
  • Help build a sustainable program.

We build people through robotics.
